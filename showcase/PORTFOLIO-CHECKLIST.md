# What to produce for Upwork, Fiverr, social and the website

One finished build gives you one asset kit. Make the kit once, then cut it down for each place. Platform rules and size limits change, so check each platform's current upload limits before you export.

## Rule zero: permission before any real client work goes public
- [ ] Written permission from the client to show this work (email is enough, keep it).
- [ ] Agreed what may be shown: name or anonymous, screenshots, numbers, a quote.
- [ ] Their data, logos and system names are removed unless they said yes to them.
- [ ] Gate 3 sign-off is done.
Until this is ticked, only demo projects on synthetic data go public, labelled as demos.

## The core kit (make these once)
1. **Case study page** (`showcase/<name>/case-study.md`). Problem, before and after, what was built, safety, proof, results, call to action.
2. **Flow Stage link.** The animated walkthrough on your own subdomain. This is your strongest asset, because a buyer can watch the system work in two minutes.
3. **Hero image.** One clean n8n canvas screenshot or a Flow Stage screenshot with a one-line headline over it.
4. **Three proof screenshots.** The alert in Slack, the record in the CRM, the log table. Use synthetic data only and blur anything that is not.
5. **Before and after graphic.** Two columns, three rows: who touches it, how long it takes, what is recorded. Measured numbers only, with how you measured them.
6. **Two-minute screen recording.** Play one scenario in the Flow Stage and talk through it using the phase SOP lines. Add captions, because many people watch with sound off.
7. **One-page PDF.** The case study cut to a page, with a contact line.
8. **Testimonial slot.** Empty until a real client gives one. Never write or edit one yourself. Ask for it at handover, in two sentences: what the problem was and what changed.
9. **Honest label.** "Demo project, synthetic data" on everything until you have a real client. When you have one, replace the demo with the client result and get the client's written permission first.

## Upwork
- **Portfolio project item:** title that names the outcome ("AI lead scoring and routing for web-form leads"), a three-sentence description, the hero image, the proof screenshots, the recording, and the Flow Stage link.
- **Project Catalog or service listing:** one fixed-scope offer taken from your complexity tiers, for example "Lead qualification automation, one form, one CRM, one chat alert". State what is in scope, the revision rounds, the timeline, and that delivery is inactive until sign-off.
- **Profile:** a headline with the outcome and the tool, and the case study as the featured work.
- **Proposals:** two sentences about their problem, the Flow Stage link as proof, one question about their form and CRM, then the fixed first step you would take.
- Do not paste client names, real lead data or credentials anywhere.

## Fiverr
- **Gig gallery:** hero image first, the before and after graphic second, a proof screenshot third, the recording as the gig video, the PDF as the gig document if the gig allows it.
- **Packages:** Basic, Standard, Advanced mapped to Simple, Standard and Advanced in `pricing-scoping.md`. Say exactly what each tier includes: apps, AI step or not, test report, handover, revision rounds.
- **Gig description:** problem, what you deliver, how it is built, what you need from the buyer, timeline.

## LinkedIn and social
- **Post 1, the story:** the problem in two lines, the Flow Stage recording, one result with how you measured it.
- **Post 2, the guardrail:** "How I stop an AI from running up a bill" (daily cap, one repair try, human fallback). People share guardrail posts.
- **Post 3, the failure path:** show the "CRM call fails" scenario and say how you find out. It builds trust.
- **Carousel:** the eight phases, one slide each, from `visualizer/SOP.md`.
- Short clips: one scenario each, 20 to 30 seconds.

## Website (aiopsexpert.com)
- A "Work" page with the case study, the Flow Stage embedded or linked, and the PDF.
- The subdomain for the Flow Stage, linked from the case study and from every platform profile.
- A simple form for "send me your form and CRM", wired to a real inbox you check.

## Keep it current
- Review the kit every quarter. Drop what no longer matches how you work.
- Re-check platform upload limits each time you export, because they change.
- Replace the demo with a real client result when you have one and permission to show it.

## Before you publish any of it
- [ ] Every number is either measured with its method written beside it, or carries the word "estimate" with its assumptions. The case study already follows this. Swap estimates for measured numbers once the AI, Slack and HubSpot credentials are connected and a hot-lead run has been timed.
- [ ] "Demo project" label is visible.
- [ ] No real customer data, names, emails, tokens or client system names in any image or recording.
- [ ] Owner has watched the recording end to end.
- [ ] The test report is signed off by the QA tester, if you show it.
- [ ] Gate 3 sign-off for anything built for a real client, plus their written permission to show it.
